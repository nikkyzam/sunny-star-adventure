For parents and grown-ups

Shine Like Stars is a free 3D game for children that runs in a web browser.

⭐ Christmas in Bethlehem: a 36-second video

A short look at the Christmas world, filmed in the game. You're welcome to share it with friends, family, your church or your school for Advent: on a phone, save the video and send it in WhatsApp or post it to your status or stories.

What children do in the game

  • Explore cheerful worlds, collect stars and coins, dress up their character and look after a pet.
  • Ride horses, donkeys and the Ferris wheel; shake trees, look under rocks and pat the animals.
  • Solve a "find what was lost" search in every big world: lost lambs (Luke 15), kittens, Noah's doves (Genesis 8), a ship's cargo (Jonah 1), David's sheep and the lost silver coins (Luke 15). A villager gives clues, and each find needs a small puzzle: searching with warmer and colder hints, cranking a well, finding a way onto a roof, or catching something that runs off. Each quest ends with a Bible verse.
  • Christmas in Bethlehem (Luke 2, Matthew 2): a starry-night town with the inn, the stable with Mary, Joseph and baby Jesus in the manger, the star overhead, and the shepherds' field with an angel. Kids help the Wise Men find their lost gold, frankincense and myrrh. It's open to everyone straight from the title screen, no verse needed.
  • Take photos: the 📸 button (or C) turns the camera round for a selfie, counts down 3-2-1 while your child strikes a pose, and makes a postcard with a short Bible verse. Saving or sharing a postcard needs the grown-up question. Photos stay on your device; nothing is uploaded, and the album is cleared when the game closes.
  • Drive, sail, fly a helicopter and do simple jobs (delivering pizza and letters, putting out pretend fires, picking carrots) to earn in-game coins.
  • Learn a short Bible memory verse (King James Version) before each new level opens.
  • Open new places with Scripture: the worlds are linked by travel gates, and each new place opens when your child types a Bible verse from memory.
  • Work through verses you choose. See "For teachers and families" below.
  • Play through three Bible stories: Noah's Ark (Genesis 6–9), Jonah and the Big Fish (the book of Jonah), and David and Goliath (1 Samuel 17). The people in each world tell the story in short lines. In David's story the giant simply falls over; nothing violent is shown.
  • Walk into the village church and the city chapel, carry food baskets to neighbors for the pastor, and find golden scrolls with Bible verses hidden around the worlds.
  • There is no fighting or scary content. Falling or bumping into a slime just sends the character back to a checkpoint.

Safety by design

  • No accounts, no passwords, no email. Nothing to sign up for.
  • No real names. Each player gets a made-up name such as "Bouncy Panda".
  • No chat. In online play, children can only send six picture stickers (👋 ⭐ 👉 🆘 🎉 ❤️). There is no typing to other players.
  • Private rooms. Online play only works with a room code that a child shares with friends they already know.
  • Coins can't be bought. There are no in-app purchases. Coins are only earned by playing.

For teachers and families: choose the verses

  • On the title screen, tap Teachers & parents: pick the verses and answer the grown-up question.
  • Pick up to 8 verses from the built-in library of about 100 (King James Version), grouped by topic. You can also type in verses of your own, in any translation.
  • Use on this device: your child gets a 📖 verse book while playing. Each verse typed from memory earns coins, and your verses replace the game's own at the end of each level.
  • Start a class: you get a room code. Up to 12 children press "Play with friends", type the code, and all get your library verses. Your verse book shows each child's made-up name and which verses they have typed from memory. Verses you typed in yourself are not sent through the game.
  • Copy the class link: the link carries all your verses, including the ones you typed. Send it to your families. Children who open it get your verses for practice at home, and in your class room their typed-in verses count on your board too.

Advertising

The game may show a short ad at natural breaks: between levels, after a "try again", or when going back to the title screen. It never shows one while your child is playing, and there are no "watch an ad for a reward" buttons. Ads are requested as child-directed and non-personalized, so they are not based on your child's interests or browsing.
